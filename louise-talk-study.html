讲者与 Inkhead 背景

  • Louise：20+ 年创意写作教学经验，伦敦 Goldsmiths 大学 Creative & Life Writing 硕士

Hello, everybody. I was just going to talk to you about how we teach children writing through Inkhead, which is a little bit different from the way it is taught in schools in England.大家好。今天想跟大家聊聊我们在 Inkhead 是如何教孩子写作的——这和英国学校里的教法有些不同。 I've been teaching creative writing for over 20 years, and I set up my company after I'd studied creative and life writing at Goldsmiths University in London.我教创意写作已经超过 20 年了，在伦敦 Goldsmiths 大学读完创意与生活写作硕士后，创办了自己的公司。 We've been collaborating with Yanci and FOGG for the past three years, and we've developed materials and courses specifically for your market, for the Singaporean and Chinese market — and it's been very successful.过去三年我们一直与 Yanci 和 FOGG 合作，专门为你们的市场——新加坡和中国市场——开发教材和课程，效果非常好。

Before setting up my company I worked as a journalist and editor.创业之前，我做过记者和编辑。 I also wrote short stories and poems, and eventually started writing a novel during my MA.我也写短篇小说和诗歌，读硕士时开始写一部小说。 But I felt that if I'd been encouraged to write for pleasure and to write fiction when I was still at school, I would have had more confidence and probably taken it further.但我意识到：如果上学时就有人鼓励我'为乐趣而写、写小说'，我会更有自信，也许会走得更远。 So I set about designing a workshop-style course for children — which meant I wasn't just standing at the front telling them how to write.所以我着手为孩子设计一种工作坊式的课程——这意味着我不是站在讲台上'教'他们怎么写。 I showed them how to build a story bit by bit; they could hear each other's stories; I would encourage them and tell them which parts of their story were good and how they could improve.我一步一步向他们展示如何搭建一个故事；孩子们能听到彼此的故事；我会鼓励他们，指出哪里写得好、哪里可以改进。 A collaborative approach.一种协作式的教学。

I did it as an experiment, and it was very successful.最初只是个实验，结果非常成功。 The children loved coming, and the group just grew and grew.孩子们喜欢来，班级越办越大。 Now these children are in their twenties — one of them wrote a novel when he was 16, another won a prestigious writing prize at Cambridge University.这批孩子如今都二十多岁了——有一个 16 岁就写了小说，另一个拿了剑桥大学的重量级写作奖。 So many of them say how much they loved our courses and how it helped their future.很多人告诉我，他们多么喜欢我们的课程、这段经历如何帮助了他们的人生。 Learning to write, and having confidence in your ability to write, will help you whatever you choose to do.学会写作、并对自己的写作能力有信心，无论将来选择什么道路都会受益。 Now the company has grown: I employ 12 tutors, we work in over 20 schools in London, and we run regular weekly clubs where the same children keep coming back to work on specific stories.如今公司已经壮大：我雇有 12 位导师，进驻伦敦 20 多所学校，开设每周固定的写作俱乐部——同一批孩子每周回来，持续打磨自己的故事。

02

阅读的力量 · Reading for Joy

13:30 – 18:00
要点
  • 引用 Cressida Cowell（《驯龙高手》作者、前英国童书桂冠作家）："文字是思想的路径"
  • 孩子后天成功的两大关键：父母参与教育 + 为乐趣而阅读
  • "为乐趣而读"的孩子在无意识中吸收词汇（imbibing words），不需要拼写测验
  • 同理：为乐趣而写 → 词汇、情节、人物、世界观全面发展
  • Louise 观察：FOGG 的孩子阅读量大、词汇量好（英语作为第二语言而言尤其突出）
Cressida Cowell 引文

"Words are pathways of thoughts. The more interesting and complicated, and the wider the vocabulary of the child, the more interesting the thought paths they can take."文字是思想的路径。孩子的词汇越有趣、越复杂、越广博，他们能走的思想路径就越有趣。 That's why we want to get them reading — because they unconsciously absorb, in the form of joyful stories, so many words, so many connections.这就是为什么我们要让孩子阅读——因为他们会在快乐的故事中，无意识地吸收大量词汇和联结。 The two key factors in a child's later economic success, let alone their happiness and educational success, are parental involvement in education, and reading for the joy of it."决定孩子日后经济成就（更不用说幸福感和学业成功）的两个关键因素是：父母对教育的参与，以及为乐趣而阅读。"

And that's the really key thing — the joy of it, not because somebody says so.关键就在'乐趣'二字——不是因为别人要求。 Children who read for the joy of it are just imbibing words: taking them on without having to have a spelling test or an explanation of what the word means.为乐趣而读的孩子是在'畅饮'词汇：不需要拼写测验、不需要词义讲解，词汇就自然被吸收了。 And I really believe the same thing happens when children are given the opportunity to write for pleasure.我坚信，当孩子有机会为乐趣而写时，同样的事情也会发生。 They'll start using their vocabularies, develop wider vocabularies, and they'll have to think about plots and characters, why people do things, and how the world is.他们会开始调用词汇、扩大词汇量，还必须思考情节和人物——人为什么这样做，世界是什么样子。

Children will come to me at the beginning worried that they don't know what to say, how to put together a story — but in a very short time they show they can express themselves really well.孩子们刚来时常担心'不知道写什么'、不会组织故事——但很短时间内，他们就展现出很强的表达能力。 And I've noticed that your children, in all the courses I've taught for FOGG, are really good readers.我也注意到，我在 FOGG 教过的所有班级里，你们的孩子都是很棒的读者。 They read avidly, and they have really good vocabularies, considering English is their second language.他们如饥似渴地阅读，而且考虑到英语是他们的第二语言，词汇量真的很出色。 I'm always really impressed.总是让我印象深刻。

03

家长如何陪读 · Active Reading

18:00 – 21:30
要点（家长可直接执行）
  • 和孩子一起读、让孩子读给你听、对他读的书表现出兴趣
  • 生词查词义 → 让孩子把定义抄进笔记本
  • 提问模板：你觉得这个角色是好是坏？接下来会发生什么？
  • Read between the lines（读懂言外之意）至关重要——英国 11+ 考试即测这个
  • 主动阅读 → 孩子学会：人物是复杂、动态的；情节会转折；场景有作用；阅读培养同理心

One way to help children develop their vocabulary is to read with them, and ask them to read to you.帮助孩子扩大词汇量的一个方法：陪他们一起读，并请他们读给你听。 Ask them questions about the books they're reading — take an interest.就他们正在读的书提问——表现出你的兴趣。 If they don't know what some words mean, look up the meanings and get them to write down the definitions in a notebook.遇到不认识的词就查词义，让孩子把定义抄进笔记本。 But it's not just about specific words: ask them about the characters.但重点不只是单词：还要问他们书里的人物。 What do they think of them? Are they good characters or bad? What's going to happen next in the story?你觉得这个角色怎么样？是好人还是坏人？故事接下来会发生什么？ Being able to read between the lines is vital.能'读懂字里行间的言外之意'，是至关重要的。

In England, in the 11-plus exam, children are asked to read an extract — usually of a Victorian novel, which is quite difficult — and they're tested on whether they can understand the text, read between the lines, predict what's going to happen, and understand the subtle references.在英国的 11+ 考试中，孩子要阅读一段选文——通常是难度不小的维多利亚时代小说——考察的正是能否读懂文本、读出言外之意、预测情节走向、理解微妙的暗示。 This kind of active reading, paying close attention, really helps children develop characters and plots in their own stories.这种全神贯注的'主动阅读'，能切实帮助孩子在自己的故事中塑造人物和情节。 They pick up that characters and stories are complex, intriguing and dynamic — not flat and one-dimensional; that things twist and turn before they come to a solution; that the setting plays an important role.他们会领悟到：人物和故事是复杂、耐人寻味且动态发展的，不是扁平单薄的；情节会几经转折才走向结局；场景设定有重要作用。 Reading also helps children develop empathy: they can understand how other people who are not like them might feel — what it feels like to be different, to be lonely, to be poor — and they can bring all this into their writing.阅读还能培养同理心：孩子能理解与自己不同的人的感受——与众不同、孤独、贫穷是什么滋味——并把这一切带进自己的写作。

04

用文字作画 · Painting with Words

21:30 – 25:00
要点（核心教学理念）
  • 写小说 = 用文字作画，但可调动所有感官：声音、气味、触感、情绪
  • 教 simile/metaphor 时讲清"为什么有效、怎么起作用"，而非"用了能拿高分"
  • 朗读自己/他人的作品 → 听出文字如何"发光"（sparkle）
  • 练习第一/第二/第三人称视角 → 让孩子自己决定"怎么讲这个故事"
  • 朗读还能自查：情节是否太复杂、是否变无聊、是否说太多

When we come to teach writing, I always begin by saying that writing fiction is like painting with words — but instead of just one sense, you can bring everything into it: the sounds, the smells, what people hear, how they feel.教写作时，我总是先说：写小说就像用文字作画——但不只是一种感官，你可以把一切都放进去：声音、气味、人物听到的、感受到的。 You are still trying to paint a picture so that the person reading will want to read it.你始终是在'画一幅画'，让读的人想读下去。 And there are techniques to doing this — things like similes and metaphors are very effective.这是有技巧的——比如明喻和暗喻就非常有效。 But we try to explain why it's effective, how it works, rather than just saying "you need to use them because you'll get a high mark if you do."但我们会解释清楚"为什么有效、它是怎么起作用的"，而不是只说"你要用，用了就能拿高分"。

We also encourage them to read their work out loud — or I often get another person in the group to read it — so they can hear their writing and understand how it makes a piece of writing sparkle.我们也鼓励孩子把作品读出来——或者我常让小组里的另一个孩子来读——这样他们能'听到'自己的文字，明白是什么让一篇文章闪闪发光。 We get them to practise writing from different points of view: first person, second person, third person, so they understand: how do I want to tell this story?我们还让他们练习不同的叙事视角：第一人称、第二人称、第三人称，让他们思考：我想怎么讲这个故事？ From one person's point of view, or with a wider perspective? These are the things writers have to think about.只从一个人的视角，还是展现更广的视野？这些正是作家动笔前要考虑的问题。 Allowing a child to form that kind of opinion — "what's best for me, how do I describe it better" — gives them a sense of confidence.让孩子形成这种判断——"什么最适合我？怎么描写更好？"——会给他们信心。 It's like they're in charge of this story; they're the ones telling it.就像这个故事由他们做主、由他们来讲。 And reading their work aloud shows them if the plot's too complicated, if it's become a bit boring, or if they've said too much.而朗读还能让他们自己发现：情节是不是太复杂、是不是变得有点无聊、是不是说得太多了。

05

工作坊教学法 · The Workshop Method

25:00 – 29:00
要点（回应家长"看不到分数"的疑虑）
  • 听同伴的故事、朗读自己的故事——看起来不像教学，但都是学习
  • Fiction 与 Nonfiction 需要不同技能；FOGG 的 essay 课教论证、说服、多角度
  • 常见误区：堆砌华丽比喻 → 最好的故事讲得非常清楚（clarity first）
  • 方法：拆解名家选段（forensic analysis），让孩子模仿作者的写法
  • 问题链：作者第一步做了什么？先写声音？再引入比喻？

The workshop method has a lot in it that is teaching children, even when it looks as if it isn't.工作坊教学法里蕴含着大量的教学内容——即便看上去不像在'上课'。 I sometimes think that some parents will want a mark, or want feedback, want to know that the child is writing for a long time — but actually they are still learning when they're listening to each other's stories, or when they're reading their stories.有些家长会想要一个分数、想要反馈、想确认孩子写了很长时间——但实际上，孩子在听同伴讲故事、在朗读自己的故事时，也都在学习。 They're taking it all in. It is all learning, and that's really important.他们在把这一切吸收进去。这全部都是学习，这一点非常重要。

Fiction and nonfiction require different skills, and we also teach the difference.虚构与非虚构写作需要不同的技能，我们也教这种差别。 We have essay courses that FOGG run which are excellent for showing children how to develop an argument, how to write a convincing essay, be persuasive, show other sides of the argument.FOGG 开设的 essay 课程非常出色，教孩子如何展开论证、写有说服力的文章、呈现论点的不同侧面。 Learning both ways of writing is really important. It's also really important that the writing is clear and makes sense.两种写作都要学。同样重要的是：文字要清晰、要讲得通。 In England a lot of children want to make it really flowery, put in lots of similes and metaphors, but it becomes too heavy.在英国，很多孩子一动笔就想写得辞藻华丽，塞进大量明喻暗喻，结果文字变得臃肿。 Over time, if children keep on writing, they realise that the best stories are told very clearly.但只要坚持写下去，孩子们会逐渐明白：最好的故事都讲得非常清楚。

So we look at good writing. We show children extracts from children's authors, and we break it down and ask them to imitate what the author is doing — which is a very powerful way of showing the craft of writing.所以我们研究好的文字。我们给孩子看童书作家的选段，把它拆解开，让孩子模仿作者的写法——这是展示'写作是一门手艺'的极有力的方式。 Writers don't just sit down and write a book and it all comes out perfectly; they're crafting it all the time.作家不是坐下来一挥而就的，他们始终在打磨。 We forensically look at different extracts: how has this author written this paragraph? What's the first thing they did?我们像做'法医分析'一样细看不同选段：这位作者是怎么写这一段的？第一步做了什么？ They might have started just with a sound, and then they bring in a simile.也许只是先写一个声音，然后才引入一个比喻。 These are extracts from authors the children might have read themselves, so it's interesting for them.这些选段往往出自孩子自己读过的作家，所以他们会很感兴趣。

06

找到孩子的声音 · Voice & Confidence

29:00 – 31:30
要点（教学态度）
  • 写作没有像数学那样的"标准答案"——鼓励孩子用自己内心的想象
  • 认真对待孩子说的每一句话，绝不说"这个不能写"
  • 经典示范："青蛙"提问链：这只青蛙会说话吗？它有什么特别的？
  • 目标：让孩子相信"任何东西都可以变成故事"

We really try and encourage children to use their voice — meaning use what's inside them, use their own imagination.我们非常注重鼓励孩子使用自己的'声音'——也就是用他们内心的东西、他们自己的想象力。 There's not a right or wrong way in writing, compared to maths.写作不像数学，没有对错之分。 If a child isn't confident about their story, that will stop them from practising and trying things out.如果孩子对自己的故事没信心，就不会去练习、去尝试。 So we take everything a child says very seriously, and we don't try and sway them. We never say "no, you can't write about that."所以孩子说的每一句话我们都认真对待，绝不去左右他们，绝不说"不行，这个不能写"。 If we do say anything, we'll say "you might find that difficult, because…" — and we keep asking them questions.最多会说"你可能会觉得这个有点难写，因为……"——然后继续用问题引导他们。

For example, I might ask "who's your main character?" and sometimes children will just say the first thing over the top of their head — something like "a frog" — thinking that you're going to say "no, you can't write a story about a frog."比如我问"你的主角是谁？"，有的孩子会脱口而出——比如"一只青蛙"——心里想着你大概会说"不行，青蛙写不成故事"。 But then I just say things like: "Can the frog talk? What's special about him?"但我只会接着问："这只青蛙会说话吗？它有什么特别之处？" So gradually they realise that whatever they say to you, you will show them that they can make a story about it.渐渐地孩子就明白了：无论他们说什么，你都会让他们看到——这个东西可以变成一个故事。

07

案例：数学男孩的蜕变

31:30 – 35:00
案例价值（course map 可复用）
  • 典型画像：数学优等生、不喜欢写作、自认写不好、投入少
  • 3–4 周课程后的对比：机械描写 → 沉浸式感官描写
  • 点评金句："He heard nothing but his own echo and the humming of insects" —— 用声音写孤独
  • 转变本质："我能做到"的信念（confidence loop）
Before · 起点

"I walked out of my house and headed for the park. It was a very big park with a playground."我走出家门，朝公园走去。那是一个很大的公园，有一个游乐场。 Wind blew onto my cheeks as I walked slowly in the park. Birds were singing, and children were playing.我在公园里慢慢走着，风吹在我的脸颊上。鸟儿在唱歌，孩子们在玩耍。 I was very bored. It is just a plain day. I sighed as I looked at my house."我觉得很无聊。这只是平淡的一天。我看着我的房子，叹了口气。" （Louise 点评：写是写了，但很机械，没有真正的生命力。）

After · 3–4 周后

"He was surprised that he had not even climbed a quarter of the mountain."他惊讶地发现，自己连四分之一的山都还没爬完。 When he reached halfway up the mountain the sun was down.爬到半山腰时，太阳已经落山了。 He took a torchlight out of his backpack, but realised it was out of battery. Oh no!他从背包里拿出手电筒，却发现没电了。糟了！ Tired and confused, nervous and pale, he looked around, but there was nothing in sight, not even a star.他又累又慌，紧张得脸色发白，环顾四周，什么也看不见，连一颗星星都没有。 'Help!' he shouted. He heard nothing but his own echo and the humming of insects.'救命！'他大喊。但他只听见自己的回声和昆虫的嗡鸣。 He began to cry, for he was only 15. Every small sound made him jump.他哭了起来，因为他只有 15 岁。任何细小的声音都让他心惊肉跳。 He stared at the black night sky."他凝视着漆黑的夜空。"

You can see from the second extract that he has now completely put himself into that situation.从第二段可以看出，他已经完全把自己代入了那个情境。 He's trying to show how this character feels, and it's really effective — much more alive.他在努力'展示'人物的感受，效果非常好——文字活起来了。 "He heard nothing but his own echo and the humming of insects" is fantastic, because he's using the sense of being alone: all he can hear is his echo and the humming of insects. Very powerful."他只听见自己的回声和昆虫的嗡鸣"这一句尤其精彩：他用声音写出了"孤身一人"的感觉。非常有力量。 He started to develop that feeling of: "Oh, I can do this. I can write. It's actually not that difficult — I just have to believe that I can show things the way I want to show them."他开始建立起这样的信念："哦，我能做到。我会写。其实没那么难——我只需要相信自己能用想要的方式把东西展示出来。"

08

从小开始 · 写作即娱乐

35:00 – 37:30
要点
  • 低龄开始的好处：学会给想法排序、掌握开头-中间-结尾的故事骨架
  • 常见问题："脑子里知道，纸上没写"——信息在脑内 ≠ 传达给读者
  • 工具：写作+绘画提示（听描述画人物 → 漏掉细节即刻可见，反证描写的重要）
  • 核心觉醒：写作的目的是娱乐读者，不是交作业拿分数

Q3 · 竞赛字数限制下怎么写

要点（可直接教学的写作工具）
  • 问题：750–800 字内很难安排情节、埋转折
  • 方法一：动笔前先做 plot——画一条时间线（开头→小事件→大事件→结尾）
  • 方法二：三格盒子（beginning / middle / end），强制精简："角色不能突然飞去墨西哥再回来"
  • 方法三（万能故事公式）：主角是谁？他想要什么？什么在阻止他？ → 结局 = 他得到了吗

Louise: You're right, it is difficult to fit in that space.
